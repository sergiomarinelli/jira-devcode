Hi, I’m Sérgio — a former developer who now works deeply with Jira Cloud.

**Jira DevCode** is my space to share practical and technical knowledge about Jira. The idea here is simple: write about the kind of things you usually learn only after dealing with real environments, real process problems, and real automation needs.

## What this blog is about

A lot of Jira content on the internet is very focused on the UI: click here, click there, create this field, add that rule.

That helps at the beginning, but after some point, it is not enough.

When you work with Jira in a more serious way, you start thinking about maintainability, scalability, governance, automation design, integrations, and how to avoid building something that becomes hard to support later.

That is the kind of content I want to publish here.

Topics on this blog include:

- Jira Automation patterns
- JQL and practical filtering strategies
- Workflows and permissions
- Jira Service Management
- REST APIs, webhooks, and integrations
- Assets / CMDB structure and organization
- Troubleshooting and edge cases

## What you can expect

Here, I try to keep the content practical and technical.

You can expect:

- deeper explanations, not only UI walkthroughs
- real examples
- snippets you can reuse
- implementation ideas that make sense in real scenarios
- notes about mistakes, limitations, and things that are easy to overlook

## Who this blog is for

This blog is for people who work with Jira in a more technical or operational way.

That includes:

- Jira admins
- Jira Service Management specialists
- analysts
- process-oriented teams
- developers who integrate with Jira
- people who want to understand how Jira works beyond the interface

## Why I created it

I created this blog because I like studying, testing, and organizing technical knowledge in a way that is useful later.

A lot of what I publish here comes from real needs, real process improvements, and things I had to understand better in order to solve problems in practice.

## Final note

My goal is to make advanced Jira knowledge more accessible, more organized, and more useful for people who want to go deeper.

If that is your case, welcome.

And if you want to exchange ideas, suggest topics, or just connect, feel free to reach out on GitHub or LinkedIn.
